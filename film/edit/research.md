Across the 591 launch films Launchscene measured, the median is 71 s, 76% have a voice and 60% have a call to action. Not found in this pass: a breakdown of a Daylight film.

## What this means for our film (my reading)

1. **About a minute, no voiceover.** Apple's median is 64 s and its AirPods film is 48 s with no narrator. Ours is 62 s and text-led.
2. **Features are told as sounds.** This suits us better than anyone: the product is a sound. The tap, the chime before a whisper, the whisper and the receipt tone are the app's real cue sounds, placed where the picture shows them.
3. **One claim per beat, one line per claim.** Five lines carry the film. Each sits alone at the bottom of the frame and holds for two reads.
4. **Withhold, then show the real thing.** Rabbit and Arc hold the product back. We hold back the working stage: 46 s of film, then the real timeline from a real (simulated) session. The film's own timeline shot sets it up.
5. **Macro, then wide, then name.** Close on one earbud, wide for the crossing, close for the arrival, and end on the name with a held frame as the music drops away.
6. **Every screen is real and legible.** The stage shots are the hub's own page, with the counts that run produced. Where the film shows something a viewer could mistake for a measurement, it carries a caption.
7. **No ask.** Apple rarely ends on a call to action. We end on the name and the line.
